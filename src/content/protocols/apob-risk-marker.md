[Attia's argument](https://peterattiamd.com/ama43/): each atherogenic particle carries one ApoB, so measuring ApoB counts the particles. Two people with identical LDL cholesterol can carry very different particle numbers, and the particle number tracks risk more closely.

It is inexpensive and not usually on a standard panel, so you generally have to ask. The [outcome data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31642874/) is observational, and what to do about a given number is a conversation with a doctor, not a number to self-treat.
